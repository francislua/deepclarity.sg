We have covered how the business works around you. This section goes one layer deeper, into how you think, reflect, and make sense of things. Some of these questions are more personal. Take your time with them.

Q106

When you look at the business today, the parts that are working well and the parts that are not, how much of that do you think traces back to your own decisions and leadership style? Are there areas where you think your own tendencies have held the business back?

Q107

Think about a time in the last year or two when something in the business did not go the way you expected, a decision that did not land, a change that did not stick, or a situation that caught you off guard. What happened, and what did you take from it?

Q108

The business touches a lot of moving parts at once, clinical operations, the team, finances, suppliers, customers, and now potentially technology. When you are trying to work out what to do next, or where to focus, how do you approach that? What does your thinking process look like?

Q109

If you think about the time and mental energy you spend on the business, how much of it goes toward the day-to-day, keeping things running, solving problems as they come up, versus stepping back and thinking about where the business is heading and what needs to change to get there? Does that balance feel right to you?

Q110

Running a business requires a very different set of skills and instincts from what most owners were originally trained for. Looking back, what has been the hardest thing to let go of or step back from, whether that is clinical work, doing things yourself, being the one with all the answers, or something else? And how have you made sense of that over time?

Q111

Where do you want to take this business in the next three to five years, not just in terms of revenue or size, but in terms of what kind of practice it is, what role you play in it, and what you want to have built? How strong is your drive to get there?

Q112

When you are facing a decision where you do not have all the information you would like, and most real business decisions are like that, how do you typically handle it? Can you give an example of a recent decision like that?
