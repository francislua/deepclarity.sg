The work

Situations I help clients navigate.

Clients usually contact me with an important decision, a recurring difficulty or a change that is proving more complex than expected. I help them bring together the relevant commercial, operational, technological and people perspectives, understand how those perspectives connect and decide what the situation requires.

01
Growth and professionalisation

Growth is changing what the business needs from its leaders and organisation.

As a business grows, the practices that supported its earlier success may place increasing demands on the owner or a small group of experienced people. Decisions take longer, responsibilities overlap and knowledge remains concentrated.

I help owners and leadership teams understand what the next stage of the business requires, where current arrangements are becoming limiting and which responsibilities, capabilities and ways of working need to develop.

Typical questions

Which decisions should remain with the owner, and which should move elsewhere?

What roles and capabilities will the next stage of growth require?

Where is the business dependent on personal knowledge or intervention?

What management practices need to become more consistent?

Succession and leadership transition

Succession involves the organisation as well as the individual leader.

A leadership transition affects decision authority, stakeholder relationships, governance, knowledge and confidence across the organisation. These matters need to be considered together if the new arrangement is to work.

I help owners and leadership teams examine the wider implications of succession, agree how leadership will operate and build the organisational conditions that support a successful transition.

03
Transformation

A major change is understood differently across the organisation.

Transformations often involve several legitimate perspectives. The business may focus on value and speed, operations on continuity, technology on delivery, and employees on how their work will change.

I help the leadership team bring these perspectives together, reach the decisions needed for coordinated action and translate those decisions into changes in leadership, accountability and work.

04
AI and the future of work

AI decisions are also decisions about work, responsibility and capability.

AI can change how information is used, where judgement sits, how roles are divided and what people need to learn. These organisational questions are easier to address before technology choices become fixed.

I help leaders connect the business purpose for AI with its operational and people implications. Where appropriate, I support the design of governance, roles, workflows and capability-building needed for responsible adoption.

05
Leadership and recurring execution problems

The same issue keeps returning despite repeated efforts to address it.

A recurring problem can indicate that different parts of the organisation are responding to different versions of the issue. It may also reflect unclear responsibility, competing priorities or an unresolved trade-off.

I help leaders examine the issue from the perspectives involved, identify what is sustaining it and agree a response that addresses the connections between leadership decisions and everyday work.

Talent and organisational capability

The organisation's people priorities need a clearer connection to the business.

Decisions about critical roles, succession and capability should reflect where the business is going and how work is changing. I help business and HR leaders bring these considerations together and develop an approach that leaders can use and sustain.

One way to begin

DeepClarity Decision Review

A focused engagement for an important decision that affects several parts of the organisation.

The review brings together relevant business information and stakeholder perspectives, identifies connections and implications that need leadership attention, and clarifies the choices and trade-offs involved.

The Output

A decision brief

The output is a decision brief that sets out:

  • the decision in its full business and organisational context
  • the perspectives and evidence that need to be considered
  • the most important implications and trade-offs
  • the decisions requiring leadership agreement
  • recommended next steps and areas requiring further design

Scope and timing depend on the complexity of the decision and the number of people involved.
