D7 — Team and Culture

Culture: What Exists and How It Got Built

Section B · Questions Q95–Q97

We'll discuss this together. Feel free to jot down initial thoughts.

Culture is often described as the way things are done around here. It shapes how your team behaves, how decisions get made, how problems get handled, and how new people fit in. Some of it is deliberate, things you have thought about and put in place. Some of it just develops over time. The questions below are about understanding what that actually looks like in your practice.

Q95

Every practice has its own way of doing things, spoken or unspoken rules about how people behave, what is expected, and what is not acceptable. How would you describe the way things work in your practice? What do people just know, without being told?

How do new staff learn what kind of place this is, what matters here, how people are expected to treat each other, and what is and is not acceptable? Is that something that gets communicated explicitly, or do people mostly pick it up by watching how things work?

Q96

Beyond day-to-day behaviour, are there any visible signals or formal and informal policies that shape how your team works together, for example, how leave is managed, how the space is set up, how disagreements or problems are handled, or any other rules (written or unwritten) that guide how things operate?

How is leave typically managed, is there a clear process, or is it handled case by case? What about the physical space, does the way it is set up reflect anything about how you want the practice to feel? When there is a performance issue or a disagreement between staff members, how does that typically get handled here?

Q97

Thinking ahead, as your practice evolves, what kind of team and culture do you think you will need to support where you want to go? Even if your plans are not fully formed yet, is there a sense of what would need to feel different from how things are today?
