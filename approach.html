The DeepClarity approach

A wider view of the decision and a practical path forward.

An engagement begins with the decision or situation the leadership team is facing. I examine it from the perspectives that matter to the business, help leaders understand how those perspectives connect and support them in deciding what should happen next.

Bringing the relevant perspectives together

Looking at the decision from the perspectives it affects

Important business decisions often cross functional boundaries. I use four broad perspectives to understand how a decision may affect the organisation and to identify the people, evidence and expertise that should inform the discussion.

These perspectives do not represent four areas of specialist advice from DeepClarity. My role is to ask the connecting questions, draw in the knowledge and expertise relevant to the situation, and help leaders understand how the different considerations affect one another.

The balance varies from one engagement to another. The purpose is to understand the decision as a whole, including the connections between its commercial intent and the organisation expected to deliver it.

Commercial

What business outcome is the decision intended to support? What assumptions about value, investment, risk and commercial trade-offs need to be examined, and whose knowledge or expertise should inform them?

Operational

How could the decision affect customers, processes, capacity, responsibilities and day-to-day delivery? What knowledge from the people closest to the work needs to inform the decision?

Technological

What systems, data or digital capabilities may be involved? Where is technical expertise needed, and how could the technology affect work, judgement and accountability?

People and organisation

What could the decision require from leaders and employees? How might it affect roles, capabilities, relationships, behaviour and the way decisions are made?

Bringing these perspectives into the same discussion helps leaders see the connections between them, identify gaps that require further expertise and make choices with a fuller understanding of what implementation will require.

What an engagement involves

From an important question to a workable response

01

Understand the situation

I begin with the business context, the decision being considered and the reasons it matters now. I review relevant information and speak with the people who hold important parts of the picture.

02

Connect the perspectives

I bring the different views together and trace the relationships between them. This reveals where assumptions differ, where consequences may have been overlooked and where the leadership team needs to make a choice.

03

Reach the necessary decisions

I work with the owners or leadership team to examine the implications and trade-offs. The result is a shared view of the issue, clear decisions and agreement on what needs to change.

04

Design the required changes

If the organisation needs further support, I work with the relevant leaders and teams to design the changes. These may involve leadership responsibilities, decision-making, governance, roles, workflows, people capability or the use of technology.

05

Put the changes into practice

Organisational capability develops through use. We test the design in the realities of the business, learn from implementation and refine it until the organisation has the confidence and internal ownership to continue.

What the work produces

The deliverables depend on the decision

The first stage usually produces a clear account of the situation, the connections between different perspectives, the choices and trade-offs requiring leadership attention, and an agreed path forward.

Further work is scoped after those choices have been made. DeepClarity may lead some areas directly, co-create others with the client, and involve internal teams, client-appointed experts or specialist partners where additional expertise is required.

Depending on the engagement, the work may produce:

  • Agreed leadership and decision responsibilities
  • Design principles and priorities for change
  • Governance and management routines
  • Role and accountability designs
  • Requirements or briefs for workflow, workforce or technology changes
  • Capability and succession plans
  • Coordinated contributions from internal teams, experts or vendors
  • Implementation plans, ownership arrangements and measures of progress

My role is to keep these parts connected. I help define what each piece of work needs to achieve, involve the right people and ensure that the solutions support the business decision and work together as a whole.

The outputs, responsibilities and timing are agreed for each phase. Organisational capability develops through implementation, so some design, specialist delivery and internal adoption may continue beyond the initial engagement.

Ways to begin

The first step is to frame the right engagement

Some situations can be clarified through a short diagnostic engagement. Others require deeper investigation or a longer period of design and implementation. The initial conversation is used to understand the decision, who is affected, what has already been attempted and what kind of support would be useful.

From there, I propose a defined first phase with a clear purpose, scope, participants and outputs. Any subsequent work is scoped around the decisions and priorities that emerge.

How Francis works

Direct involvement throughout the engagement

I lead the inquiry, leadership conversations, synthesis and recommendations personally. Clients work with the same adviser from the first conversation through design and implementation.

My role is to contribute an independent view, ask questions that widen the discussion and help leaders connect commercial intent with organisational reality. I work closely with internal leaders and specialists, since they hold knowledge that any workable solution must reflect.

Every engagement includes explicit outcomes and review points. When implementation support is required, the aim is to develop sufficient internal ownership for the organisation to sustain and improve the work.
